  1. Design the initial approach, goals, and requirements

    • Goal
    • Mission
    • Approach
    • Initial procedures
    • Bylaws

  2. Organize founding committees

    • Strategy
    • Services
    • Legal, rules, and bylaws
    • Membership
    • Outreach

  3. Set the overall architecture and goals for the community co-op

    • Design initial services
    • Design and implement the initial co-ownership structure
    • Build budget requirements
    • Conduct a funding operation

  4. Present a startup plan to the initial founding group

    Bring the early ideas and groundwork together in a plan the founding group can review and build on.

  5. Meet with the overall group to workshop initiatives and refine goals

    Brainstorm initial services

    • Access control
    • Mail and package drop-off point
    • Circularity hub
    • Bike repair hub
    • Physical item library
    • And more

    Set the technical approach to management

    • Organizational structure and ownership model
    • Documentation needed
    • Resources and fiduciary tools needed
    • Community engagement strategy
    • Organizational support and sponsorship strategy
    • Engagement and social media strategy

  6. Set a cadence for ongoing services review

    Make regular space to review how services are working and where they can grow.

  7. Build out and procure services

    Turn the community's priorities into useful, shared services.

  8. Seek new members and do outreach

    Invite more neighbors to take part, contribute ideas, and help shape the co-op.

  9. Run the co-op!

    Keep ownership, decision-making, and community needs at the heart of the work.

A stronger neighborhood, built together

Each step makes room for neighbors to shape shared services, shared ownership, and a co-op that belongs to the community.
